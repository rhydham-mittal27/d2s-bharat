\section{Approach}\label{sec:approach}

The analysis moves from \emph{descriptive} (what the market and employees look like) to
\emph{diagnostic} (which skills and traits are associated with pay and success) to
\emph{prescriptive} (what to teach first). Figure~\ref{fig:flow} shows the overall flow.

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{\input{figures/flow}}
\caption{Analytical flow: four SAS datasets $\rightarrow$ cleaning and derivation $\rightarrow$
three diagnostic questions $\rightarrow$ one prescriptive decision.}\label{fig:flow}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Overall flow.} The work proceeds in five stages, each feeding the next:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Audit and prepare} all four files (Section~\ref{sec:data}): repair, derive, de-duplicate
        and reduce until every variable needed by an RQ is clean and analysable.
  \item \textbf{Describe the market} (RQ1): what is demanded, and what is paid more once
        experience and role are held constant.
  \item \textbf{Diagnose the employees} (RQ2, RQ3): which measured skills and traits separate
        high from low outcomes, with tests, effect sizes and validated models.
  \item \textbf{Triangulate}: place the five JDS skill areas in the job market so that market and
        employer evidence can be compared on the same scale.
  \item \textbf{Prescribe} (RQ4): turn gaps and evidence weights into an optimal, budget-feasible
        training plan and test how robust it is.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Motivation and reasons for this approach.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Decision first.} The stakeholders (training institutions) need an action, not only
        a description, so the analysis is designed backwards from the RQ4 decision: every
        quantity the optimiser needs (gaps, value of a skill) is produced by an earlier stage.
  \item \emph{Triangulation over a single source.} Job postings show what employers \emph{ask
        for}; JDS shows what employers \emph{reward} after hiring. Neither alone is reliable, so a
        skill is prioritised only when the sources agree, and disagreements (storytelling) are
        reported as findings.
  \item \emph{Methods matched to the data, not the other way round.} Salary comes in ordered bands,
        JDS scores sit at a ceiling and JDS/SDS are small; the methods below were chosen for these
        properties. Complex models (gradient boosting, neural networks) were not used because with
        139--161 people they would add variance without adding explanation.
  \item \emph{Transparency.} Every step is scripted and every assumption (notably RQ4 course
        costs) is listed, so a jury or an institution can re-run the analysis with its own numbers.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Alternatives considered and rejected.} Linear regression on salary midpoints (treats
coarse bands as exact amounts); extracting skills from descriptions by meaning (tested, F1 6.8\%,
Section~\ref{sec:skills}); deleting re-used JDS/SDS IDs (would lose 3--11\% of people without
evidence that they are duplicates); and a linear-programming plan (proposes fractional courses,
Section~\ref{sec:rq4}).

\textbf{Why these methods.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rank-based tests and effect sizes} (Mann--Whitney U \citep{mannwhitney1947},
        rank-biserial correlation \citep{kerby2014}) because the JDS 1--5 scores are bounded and
        heavily skewed towards the ceiling; Holm correction \citep{holm1979} controls the five
        tests per dataset. Bootstrap CIs \citep{efron1993} quantify uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{L2-regularised logistic regression and depth-limited trees} \citep{breiman1984}
        for the small JDS/SDS samples. With five predictors the smaller class gives 13.2 (JDS) and
        15.2 (SDS) events per predictor, a favourable ratio \citep{peduzzi1996,vittinghoff2007};
        complexity is still kept deliberately low and every model is scored by repeated stratified
        5-fold cross-validation (20 repeats) against a majority-class baseline, with a label-permutation
        test of the AUC.
  \item \textbf{Ordinal logistic regression} \citep{mccullagh1980} for pay, because salary is
        given in six ordered bands, not exact amounts; it lets us control for experience and role
        family. Benjamini--Hochberg FDR \citep{benjamini1995} controls the 30--40 skills tested.
  \item \textbf{Association rules} \citep{agrawal1994} reveal skill bundles that should be taught
        together.
  \item \textbf{Integer optimisation} (CP-SAT \citep{cpsat}) because courses are all-or-nothing,
        have batch sizes and prerequisites; a linear relaxation would propose fractional courses.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Tools.} Python 3.13 with pandas, scikit-learn \citep{pedregosa2011}, statsmodels
\citep{seabold2010}, sentence-transformers \citep{reimers2019} (\texttt{bge-small-en-v1.5}
\citep{xiao2023bge}) and OR-Tools; all steps are scripted and seeded
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). Equivalent SAS code for the core statistics is provided for SAS Viya
for Learners \citep{sasvfl} (Appendix~\ref{app:sas}).
