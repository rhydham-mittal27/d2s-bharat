RQ4 is done: it produces a **proven-optimal training plan** built on the RQ1–RQ3 evidence, and all 49 tests pass. The plan's choices are robust, but the costs, seats and trainer-hours are our assumptions, so the exact seat counts are illustrative.

## The plan (₹4 lakh budget, 200 trainer-hours)
| Course | Seats | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Applied Statistics & Probability | 60 | ₹0.90 L |
| SAS Programming on SAS Viya for Learners | 60 | ₹0.45 L |
| Python & SQL for Analytics | 35 | ₹0.88 L |
| Machine Learning Foundations | 27 | ₹1.13 L |
| Dashboards & Storytelling (Power BI) | 60 | ₹0.64 L |
| **Total** | **242** | **₹3.995 L**, 186 hours |

**Share of each skill gap closed:**
- coding 100%
- maths & stats 94%
- dashboards & storytelling 93%
- AI & ML 38%
- big data 0%

## Main findings
1. **Robust core.** Statistics, SAS, Python & SQL and ML foundations are chosen in **all 9 evidence scenarios** (3 weightings × low/point/high confidence bounds). Power BI storytelling is chosen in 7 of 9.
2. **Money is the limit at ₹4 L, then trainers.** An extra ₹1 lakh adds **+11.9% impact**, and the approximate and exact sensitivity checks agree (0.0235 vs. 0.0249 per rupee). **Above about ₹4.5 L, more money buys nothing** with 200 trainer-hours: each extra trainer-hour is worth +59 impact. With 400 hours, Big Data and DL/NLP join the plan. **Recommendation: add trainers, or share them across institutions, before adding budget.**
3. **Each excluded course has a stated reason.** For example, forcing in DL/NLP costs −35% impact, because GPU costs and its ML prerequisite take money away from the foundations.
4. **Diminishing returns:** the gains are steep up to about ₹2.2 L (the foundation courses), then flatten.

The SAS course is favoured partly because SAS Viya for Learners has no per-seat cost. The report states this.

**Personality traits (RQ3) aren't treated as courses**, because personality is fairly stable. They feed the recommendations for mentoring and role allocation instead.
