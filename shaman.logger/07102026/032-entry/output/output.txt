**Technical design added to `features.md`**
- **§6.3 Uncertainty-aware planning (V1):** the forecast's `lo`/`point`/`hi` bands become scenarios. Four objective modes:
  - expected (best on average)
  - worst-case (safest if demand disappoints)
  - **blended**, the default: average with downside protection, controlled by a slider
  - min-max regret (never far worse than the best plan for any scenario)
  
  Two other pieces:
  - **Market absorption:** training more people than the market can hire in a low scenario counts as waste, not as success.
  - **"Safe core":** the courses that stay in the plan under every scenario.
- **§6.4 Decision backtesting (V1):** pick a past cutoff date and plan using only data up to that date. Compare against four baselines:
  - top-gap-first
  - most-popular-course
  - greedy value-per-rupee
  - random
  
  Also compare against an *oracle* plan made with the real future demand, to show the best that was achievable. Score coverage, oversupply, cost per learner and % of oracle achieved. It includes rules against leaking future data and against unlabelled synthetic numbers.
- **§6.5 Multi-institution pooling (V2+):** seats at one college allocated to students from another, shared trainers (with travel time), and which students can reach which college. Plus cost sharing, a fairness floor per institution, and the **pooling gain**: pooling is only suggested when it beats each college planning alone.
- **Delivery plan:** robust planning and backtesting go to the pilot phase (V1); pooling goes to scale (V2+).

**What each one needs**
- **Uncertainty-aware planning:** the cheapest to build next. The forecaster already produces the bands, and the engine only needs one value variable per scenario.
- **Backtesting:** blocked on data, not code. It needs 2–3 years of time-stamped job postings, which we don't have yet. Until then, only labelled synthetic tests are possible.
