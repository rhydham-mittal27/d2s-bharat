I’d rate this **9.2/10** as an analytics objective / hackathon problem statement.

It is unusually strong because it connects the analysis to an actual decision rather than stopping at “find patterns in the data.” The core objective is clear: identify skills/traits associated with demand, pay, and success, then convert that evidence into a constrained training plan. analytics_objective

The strongest parts:

- **Excellent research structure — 9.5/10.** RQ1 → RQ2 → RQ3 → RQ4 has a logical progression from market evidence → junior outcomes → senior outcomes → actionable intervention. analytics_objective
- **Strong methodological alignment — 9.5/10.** Cleaning/normalisation for RQ1, statistical tests and regression/tree models for RQ2–3, and CP-SAT optimisation for RQ4 are appropriately matched to the questions. analytics_objective analytics_objective
- **Very good statistical discipline — 9.5/10.** Explicitly stating that findings are associations rather than causal effects, plus cross-validation, effect sizes and confidence intervals, is a major strength. analytics_objective
- **Excellent decision orientation — 9.5/10.** The final optimisation question makes the project substantially more valuable than a conventional exploratory analytics project. analytics_objective
- **Good stakeholder definition — 9/10.** You identify institutions, students, employers and policy/CSR programmes and give each a concrete output. analytics_objective
- **Strong success criteria — 9/10.** Requiring quantified answers and comparing predictive models against a majority-class baseline is much better than vague “build an accurate model” language. analytics_objective

The main weakness is that **RQ2/RQ3 may be statistically ambitious relative to the sample sizes**: 139 junior observations and 161 senior observations. You acknowledge this, which is good, but logistic regression + decision tree + cross-validation can still produce unstable estimates with small samples, especially depending on the number of predictors. analytics_objective

I’d also tighten one conceptual point: **“personal traits distinguish successful senior data scientists”** could sound causal or overly deterministic. Your scope later correctly says these are associations, so the wording could be made consistent from the beginning.

Overall, though, this looks like a **serious competition-grade analytics framing**, not a generic student project. The biggest differentiator is RQ4: you are not merely asking *“what skills matter?”* but *“given limited money, trainers and seats, what should an institution actually teach first?”* That gives the entire analysis a decision-science backbone.

**For a SAS / Build for Bharat hackathon: ~9.3/10.**  
**For an academic research proposal: ~8.8/10.**  
**For a normal college analytics project: ~9.7/10.**
