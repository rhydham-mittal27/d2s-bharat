## Key findings
1. **Each extra year of required experience multiplies the odds of a higher salary band by 1.66.** Skill effects are measured *on top of* that.
2. **Higher-paid skills in data roles:**
   - product management 2.95×
   - NLP 1.74×
   - predictive modelling 1.67×
   - SAS 1.42×
   - statistics 1.43×
   - Tableau and BI 1.41×

   **Lower-paid:** MIS 0.25× and "communication" 0.70×. These mark support roles.
3. **Skills come in bundles:** hive → Hadoop (75% of the time), Scala → Spark (72%), deep learning ↔ NLP. Teach them together.
4. **The combined RQ1–RQ2 table is the strongest result:**
   - **Maths & stats is consistently valuable:** paid more in the market (1.56×) *and* the strongest predictor of junior salary hikes (3.0×).
   - **Coding is the baseline:** it appears in 41% of data postings.
   - **Dashboards & storytelling is the gap:** it gets no clear premium in job postings (1.14, not significant) but is the #2 predictor of junior hikes (2.7×). That's what institutions should add.
5. **DataScience Jobs:**
   - Experience and salary are strongly linked (ρ = 0.63).
   - The top 10 firms hold 35% of openings.
   - Volume is concentrated in IT-services firms, so the job-weighted average salary (9.7 L) is below the typical company's (11.9 L).

## Problems found and handled
- **The "storytelling" result depended on its definition.** Counting "reporting" and "MIS" as storytelling flipped the market effect to 0.79. I use the narrow definition (visualisation and BI tools), keep the other as a robustness check, and explain both in the report.
- **"Analytical" was mapped to "analytical chemistry",** which distorted the pay model. Fixed with a short, documented correction list.
- **Microsoft BI** looked significant in an earlier run but isn't in the final one, so it's out of the report.
- **A few tag merges are imperfect** (off-page vs. on-page SEO). They affect 1.3% of tag uses and are noted as a limitation.
