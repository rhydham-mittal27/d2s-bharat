I wrote the analytics objective in LaTeX and compiled it to a one-page PDF in Times 12 pt, the font the Round-2 report requires: `reports/objective/analytics_objective.pdf`, with source in `analytics_objective.tex`. It's marked *"Draft for team agreement"*.

**The objective, as written:**
> *To identify the technical skills and personality traits most strongly associated with labour-market demand, pay and on-the-job success for data-science professionals, and to translate this evidence into a budget-constrained, explainable training plan that tells an institution what to teach first.*

**It's broken into four research questions, so all four SAS files are used:**

| RQ | Question | Data | Main methods |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ1 Demand & pay | Which skills and roles are most demanded and best paid? | Analytics Jobs (14,840) + DataScience Jobs (1,602 rows, 93,005 openings) | Cleaning, skill normalisation, descriptive stats, non-parametric tests |
| RQ2 Junior growth | Which skill areas separate high vs. low salary hike? | JDS (139) | Tests, correlation, logistic regression, decision tree, cross-validation |
| RQ3 Senior success | Which Big Five traits separate high vs. low success? | SDS (161) | Same as RQ2 |
| RQ4 Decision | What should an institution teach first within its budget? | Evidence from RQ1–3 | CP-SAT optimisation, sensitivity analysis |

The page also covers:
- **Scope and assumptions:** only the SAS data is analysed, ESCO/O\*NET are used only as a vocabulary, results are associations rather than causes, and the small samples get cross-validation and confidence intervals.
- **Stakeholders:** institutions, students, and employers or policy/CSR programmes.
- **Success criteria:** models must beat a majority-class baseline on cross-validated accuracy and AUC.

**Points for the team to decide:**
