## Where we're off track or missing pieces ⚠️

1. **JDS and SDS are unused.** These are the only files with outcome labels (salary hike, success), so they're the natural place for statistics and ML:
   - group comparisons (t-test or Mann-Whitney U)
   - correlations
   - logistic regression and a decision tree, with cross-validation
   - which skills or traits drive the outcome

   Without them, the 30-mark analysis section is mostly descriptive.
2. **There's no single written analytics objective tying all four files together**, and the brief stresses that this matters most. One possible version:
   > *"Which skills does the data-science job market demand and pay for (job files), which skills drive early-career growth (JDS) and which traits drive senior success (SDS), and therefore what should an institution teach first within its budget (optimization)?"*

   That's one story, from market to individual to decision, and it's essentially D2S Bharat applied to this data.
3. **We've built a lot of software that the judges won't score directly**: workers, the SLM plan, the Next.js design, the XAI framework. Keep it for Build for Bharat if that's judged separately; for this rubric, only what shows up in the report and slides counts.
4. **No SAS Viya work yet.** It isn't mandatory ("technology agnostic"), but this is a SAS-run hackathon and slide 21 suggests a VA report and a Model Studio model. A VA dashboard and one Model Studio model would likely help with the jury.
5. **The report hasn't been started.** 20–25 pages in their structure (Problem → Approach → Exploration → Analysis → Results → Implications) is real work.
6. **Some caution needed:**
   - JDS has only **139 rows** and SDS **161**: use cross-validation, keep models simple, and don't over-claim.
   - The Analytics Jobs file includes many non-data-science roles (digital marketing, accounting): either filter to data-science roles or say clearly why they're kept.
   - Using ESCO/O\*NET and a pretrained model is probably fine since any tool is allowed, but **confirm with the organisers** given the "no data transfer to the outside world" rule.
