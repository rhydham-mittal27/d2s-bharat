## What this hackathon is
It's the **SAS CU Hackathon** (SAS with Chandigarh University), and it's **a different competition from Build for Bharat**. It's a data-analysis contest: SAS supplies the datasets and you set your own analytics objective. The theme is *data science jobs, skills and the personality traits of data scientists*.

**Their tip:** "Everything depends on your problem identification. Do not haste." They expect real time spent on framing the objective.

## The 4 datasets (sample data, 2024–25)
| File | Rows | What's in it | Data issues I found |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Analytics Jobs.csv** | 15,841 | experience, job description, designation, job type, **key skills**, location, salary band | 22% of descriptions empty (3,508); job type 76% empty and inconsistent ("Analytics" / "analytics" / "ANALYTICS"); salary bands stored as text ("10to15") |
| **DataScience Jobs.csv** | 1,602 | company, job title, minimum experience, average/min/max salary, number of jobs | salaries stored as text ("7.8L") |
| **JDS Skill Traits.xlsx** (junior data scientists) | 139 | 1–5 scores: big data, maths-stats, coding, AI/ML, dashboard & storytelling → **salary hike (1 = high / 0 = low)** | 53% high, so the two classes are balanced; small sample |
| **SDS Personality Traits.xlsx** (senior data scientists) | 161 | Big Five scores (neuroticism, extraversion, openness, agreeableness, conscientiousness) → **success (1 = high / 0 = low)** | 53% high; small sample; one column name contains spaces |

The organisers themselves warn that the data contains misspellings, outliers and masked entries.

## How it's judged
**Round 2: written report, 70% of the final score.** Word format, 20–25 pages plus appendix, Times New Roman 12 pt. Marking (out of 100):

| Section | Marks |
|---|---|
| Problem definition | 10 |
| Approach | 15 |
| **Data exploration and preparation** | **25** |
| **Data analysis** (statistics / data mining / machine learning) | **30** |
| Results and conclusions | 10 |
| Implications for stakeholders | 10 |

**Round 3: presentation, 30%, top 8 teams.** 15–25 PowerPoint slides, 10 minutes, plus 3–5 minutes of jury Q&A. Marking (out of 100):

| Section | Marks |
|---|---|
| Communication | 25 |
| Jury Q&A | 25 |
| Storyline | 20 |
| Conclusions | 15 |
| Graphics | 10 |
| Slide management | 5 |

**Tools:** any tool is allowed (Python, R, Julia, Scala, SAS). Using SAS software means uploading the data to SAS Viya for Learners, which needs a SAS profile and a university email address. They suggest building a SAS Visual Analytics report and a SAS Model Studio model. Excel is "discouraged", allowed only for preliminary work.

**Rules to watch:**
- "No e-mail access or data transfers to outside world."
- No cross-team discussion.
- The jury's decision is final.

## How it connects to our work
- Our D2S skill-extraction pipeline fits **Analytics Jobs.csv** directly. Its `key_skills` column works as recruiter tags, like Naukri's, so we could map 15,841 posts to ESCO/O\*NET skills and compare demand by role, salary band and city.
- One possible objective linking all four files: *which skills does the market pay for (job data), which skills predict a salary hike for juniors (JDS), and which traits predict success for seniors (SDS)?* That gives a single story, from market demand to individual success. It's a suggestion; the framing is your call.
